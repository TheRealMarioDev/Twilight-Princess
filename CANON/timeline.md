# Twilight Princess Canon Timeline — Campaign Reference

## Timeline position
Twilight Princess occurs in the Child Timeline following the Child Ending of Ocarina of Time. This campaign treats the game's established chronology as the baseline canon.

## Campaign opening
**Starting point:** Two in-world days before Link is scheduled to leave Ordon for Hyrule.

The game's opening establishes Link and Rusl at Ordon Spring. Rusl asks Link to deliver a gift to the Royal Family of Hyrule **the day after tomorrow**, making the campaign's starting point the first evening of the two-day countdown. Link is a young adult ranch worker in Ordon Village. The following day contains the village activities involving the children, fishing, Sera's cat, the slingshot, the wooden sword, sword practice, and Talo's pursuit of the monkey. The next morning, immediately before Link's scheduled departure, Link herds the goats again; Ilia notices Epona's injury and takes her to Ordon Spring, and the Twilight-related inciting sequence follows.

## Opening schedule / playable windows

### DAY -2 — First evening of the countdown
**Canon anchor:** Rusl has just asked Link to make the Hyrule delivery the day after tomorrow. The opening conversation has concluded.

**Fixed facts:**
- Link lives and works in Ordon Village.
- Link is familiar with the villagers and local countryside.
- Epona is Link's horse.
- Rusl has entrusted Link with the Hyrule delivery.
- The delivery is scheduled for the day after tomorrow.
- The Twilight crisis is not yet an ordinary known fact to Link.

**Player freedom:** High. This is intentionally a campaign-created playable window. Link can work, explore, visit villagers, spend time with friends, ride or care for Epona, practice skills, pursue ordinary errands, or initiate conversations. The Game Master should not force the game's next tutorial scene prematurely.

**Canon pressure:** Low. Do not introduce future revelations simply to create drama.

### DAY -1 — The day before departure
**Canon anchors:** The game's established early activities become available: the children wake Link, Colin's fishing-rod thread begins, Link obtains the fishing rod and catches a fish for Sera's cat, receives a bottle, works toward the slingshot, shows the children the slingshot, receives the wooden sword, demonstrates sword techniques, and the children pursue the monkey. Link then rescues Talo and the monkey.

**Player freedom:** High between canon anchors. The player may perform additional activities and have conversations as long as they fit the time, location, character knowledge, and physical circumstances.

**Canon pressure:** Moderate. The day should naturally progress toward the established monkey/Talo sequence, but player-created scenes can happen around it.

### DAY 0 — Scheduled departure / Twilight incident
**Canon anchors:** Link is expected to leave for Hyrule. Fado asks him to finish the goat work; Link herds the goats. Ilia notices an injury to Epona and takes her to Ordon Spring. Colin, Talo, and Malo become involved; Talo has the wooden sword. Link reaches the spring. Bulblins attack, King Bulblin takes Colin and Ilia, and the Twilight inciting sequence follows.

**Player freedom:** Moderate before the attack, then increasingly constrained by the unfolding crisis. The campaign should preserve the game's causal sequence while incorporating player-established relationships and dialogue.

## Ordered main-story sequence
1. Ordon Village introduction and Rusl's request.
2. Day -1 village activities: children, fishing rod, Sera's cat/bottle, slingshot, wooden sword, combat practice, monkey chase, rescue of Talo and the monkey.
3. Day 0 goat herding; Ilia takes Epona to Ordon Spring; Colin asks Link for help; Talo has Link's wooden sword; Link reaches Ordon Spring.
4. Bulblins attack; Colin and Ilia are taken; Twilight reaches Ordon; Link is pulled into Twilight and transformed into a wolf; he is imprisoned and encounters Midna.
5. Link escapes with Midna; returns to Ordona and obtains the Ordon Sword and Ordon Shield.
6. Link and Midna enter Twilight-covered Faron Woods; meet the diminished Light Spirit Faron and restore the Faron Province's light by collecting Tears of Light.
7. Link regains Hylian form and learns of his role as the chosen hero; receives the Hero's Clothes and is directed toward the Forest Temple.
8. Forest Temple: Link and Midna recover the first Fused Shadow and defeat Diababa.
9. Faron directs them toward the Eldin Province.
10. Eldin Province/Kakariko Village in Twilight: restore the Eldin Light Spirit with Tears of Light; Link reunites with the Ordon children, but Ilia remains missing.
11. Death Mountain/Goron Mines: Link learns about the Gorons, receives the Iron Boots through Mayor Bo, defeats Gor Coron in sumo, enters the Goron Mines, defeats Fyrus/Darbus, and obtains the second Fused Shadow.
12. Lanayru Province in Twilight: restore Lanayru's light and pursue the story toward Hyrule Castle Town and Lake Hylia.
13. Lakebed Temple: defeat the dungeon boss and obtain the third Fused Shadow.
14. Sacred Grove/Master Sword: Link is guided toward the Sacred Grove and obtains the Master Sword; later story developments permit progression toward the Arbiter's Grounds.
15. Gerudo Desert/Arbiter's Grounds: uncover the history surrounding the Twilit Mirror and confront the dungeon's threats.
16. Snowpeak: meet Yeto/Yeta and explore Snowpeak Ruins; obtain another shard of the Mirror of Twilight.
17. Sacred Grove/Temple of Time: return to the Sacred Grove, gain access to the Temple of Time, and progress through the dungeon and associated time-travel sequence.
18. City in the Sky: pursue the final major dungeon and obtain the final required progression item.
19. Palace of Twilight: enter the Twilight Realm and confront Zant.
20. Hyrule Castle: final confrontation with Ganondorf and resolution of the central conflict.

## Canon chronology rule
The numbered sequence is the high-level spine. Optional exploration, sidequests, conversations, travel, and player-created scenes may occur between appropriate main-story beats. Player actions can create divergences, but the original canon event should remain documented as the baseline rather than being silently rewritten.

## Time and scene rules
- The campaign uses an explicit in-world day/time marker rather than treating scenes as timeless.
- A scene may advance time when appropriate; the Game Master should not arbitrarily skip hours or days when the player is actively engaging with events.
- NPC schedules should matter. Characters should not be assumed to be available anywhere at any time.
- Canon events are anchors, not teleporters: Link still has to plausibly travel and act between them.
- The two pre-Twilight days are deliberately more flexible than the game's original playable sequence. They exist to establish relationships and player-created history without automatically displacing the canon sequence.
- If a player action makes a canon event impossible, record the divergence and its cause instead of forcing the original scene to happen unchanged.
